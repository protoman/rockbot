What to draw
  1. 3 shades + black, transparent where the background shows through. Keep interior black opaque — on the moving platform that black is the gaps between its segments, and making it transparent would show the sky through them.
  2. Strip = N frames × 16 px wide, 16 tall, with N a multiple of 3 (3 frames is enough; 6 if you want more steps).
  3. Frame k+1 = frame k with the three shades advanced one step, always the same direction. Draw one frame, make the rest by rotating the shades — that is the whole technique. It also means the shades must form a repeating band in the art; the motion appears because the band rotates.
  4. Don't chase exact RGBs: keep the three shades clearly ordered lightest → darkest, that order is my rotation direction.

========================================================================================================================================================
Palette: you don't pick one, and no swatch tile needed
========================================================================================================================================================
The converter takes the three brightest colours of the art as the band, in luminosity order, folds anything else into the nearest one,
and places those three in the free PAL3 entries of each stage. Free entries I measured per stage: 1 → 7–9, 2 → 6, 3 → 12, 6 → 10, 8 → 4, 0 → none (its lightbulb stays on tile frames).
So: draw in true colour, no palette list, no per-stage decisions. The extra-tile swatch stays in reserve for the one case it actually solves — when you want a colour the art doesn't contain, or a rotation order that isn't light→dark. I'll add that parser only when we hit such a block.
Per-stage hue variation (green water in the jungle, blue in the cave) is free too: the art stays identical, I just write different RGBs into the pool.
